**_As AI becomes capable of doing more, how do we decide where it actually belongs, and where simpler approaches are still the better choice?_**

AI can now help teams write code, design systems, work with data and build increasingly capable products. But being able to use AI somewhere doesn't necessarily mean that we should.

Sometimes the harder engineering decision is deciding where **not** to introduce it: keeping an architecture simple rather than adding unnecessary complexity, or using conventional models where outputs need to remain calibrated and auditable while reserving an LLM for the parts where language actually helps.

For this edition of AI Wednesdays, we're looking at two systems built with that kind of deliberate restraint: a portfolio of SME SaaS applications designed around a deliberately minimal architecture, and an industrial property intelligence system that separates conventional machine learning from the parts of the product where an LLM adds value.

---

## _More About The Sharings_

**_Jojo Boquiren (Independent App Developer)_** will share on **_“A Minimalist Architecture for SME SaaS Applications on Google Cloud.”_**

> What do you actually need to build and operate a portfolio of SaaS applications, and what can you deliberately leave out? Jojo will unpack how one developer runs multi-tenant SME applications using Vue, Go, Cloud Run and Postgres with row-level security, including the architectural decisions behind the stack and the long list of things he chose not to build.
> 
> Explore what simplicity looks like as an engineering choice, where additional infrastructure and abstraction may not be necessary, and how increasingly capable AI development tools affect the decisions we make about the systems underneath them. _(Technical Level: 200)_

**_Nitesh Kumar (Co-founder & CTO, RE-LIVE)_** will share on **_“Numbers from Models, Words from LLMs”_**

> Where should an LLM sit in a system when some outputs need to remain calibrated, explainable and auditable? Nitesh will unpack the technical architecture behind an industrial and logistics property intelligence system built from Singapore public data, from joining property records across multiple sources to the machine-learning models used for valuation.
> 
> Explore why the team keeps valuation within conventional models, including gradient-boosted trees, conformal ranges, SHAP explanations and comparable scoring, while using an LLM where language is useful: retrieving and explaining results without generating the underlying numbers itself.
> 
> Nitesh will also share how the assistant is kept grounded in verified data, and close with the gaps in Singapore's public property record and what better data could unlock. _(Technical Level: 200)_
