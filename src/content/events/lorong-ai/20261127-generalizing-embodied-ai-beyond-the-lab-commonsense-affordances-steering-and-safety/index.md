_What does it take for embodied AI to work reliably beyond controlled environments?_

Dr. Cheston Tan will explore how embodied AI systems can **generalize beyond the lab**, where robots must contend with unfamiliar situations, physical uncertainty, and failures that are difficult to anticipate during development.

Drawing from published and ongoing work at A\*_STAR's Institute of Advanced Intelligence & Computing (A\*_STAR IAIC), he'll explore four complementary capabilities for more robust generalization:

1.  **Physical commonsense and intuitive physics**
    
2.  **Affordance understanding**
    
3.  **Deployment-time steering**
    
4.  **Safety and failure recovery**
    

He'll discuss how these capabilities can help embodied AI systems better understand and adapt to the physical world, respond when things do not go as expected, and move towards more reliable operation in open and unpredictable environments.

## **_More About the Speaker_**

![User Uploaded Image](https://images.lumacdn.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto,fit=cover,dpr=2,quality=75,width=800,height=800/uploads/3y/9994461d-74d3-4e06-ac6b-bc1051a96904.png)

**_[Dr. Cheston Tan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cheston-tan?utm_source=luma)_** is a Senior Principal Scientist and Head of Embodied AI & Robotics at A\*_STAR's Institute of Advanced Intelligence & Computing (A\*_STAR IAIC).

He obtained his PhD from the Department of Brain and Cognitive Sciences at MIT and his BSc with Highest Honours from the Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at UC Berkeley. He serves as Senior Area Chair for NeurIPS and ICML and Area Chair for ICLR, and was previously an Associate Editor for IEEE Transactions on Cognitive and Developmental Systems.

His awards and honours include the Ubiquitous Robotics conference Best Application Paper, CVPR Outstanding Reviewer, STAR Mentor Award and NeurIPS Travel Award. At MIT, he received the Mark Gorenberg Graduate Student Fellowship and Walle Nauta Award for Excellence in Graduate Teaching, and at UC Berkeley, the Chancellor Tien Award for Engineering and Computer Science.
