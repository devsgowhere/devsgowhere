**_What changes when building AI stops being an abstract technical problem and starts happening inside a real organisation?_**

Real-world AI projects rarely begin with a neatly defined problem or a clear solution. Teams have to work out what should actually be built, make technical decisions within existing constraints, adapt as they learn more about the problem, and eventually get something working in production.

But deployment isn't necessarily the end of that process. Working closely on real problems can also reveal recurring patterns: which challenges need a local fix, which are worth turning into reusable tools or workflows, and what those experiences can teach teams about the products and models they're building.

For this edition of AI Wednesdays, we're looking at **Forward Deployed Engineering** from two sides of that process: getting an ambiguous industry problem into production within a client's environment, and what teams learn across deployments once those systems meet the real world.

## _More About The Sharings_

**_Howe Lau (AI Singapore)_** will share on **_“From Scoping to Handover: Building AI That Clients Can Actually Own.”_**

> What changes when the team deploying an AI system has to work within the client's existing technology stack, and the client ultimately needs to own and maintain what gets built? Drawing on two anonymised industry deployments, Howe will unpack the journey from initial problem scoping through to the first production deployment, including how teams decide what to build first and the decisions and trade-offs involved in getting a system live within a client's environment.
> 
> Explore how existing technical constraints, client ownership and long-term maintainability shape engineering decisions during deployment, and what it takes to build something that can eventually be handed over to the team that will operate it. _(Technical Level: TBC)_

**_Mounir Benzineb (Forward Deployed Engineer, OpenAI)_** will share on **_“The Work That Starts After Deployment: Lessons from the Frontier.”_**

> What do teams learn once AI systems start meeting real-world problems? Drawing from his work as a Forward Deployed Engineer, Mounir will explore how lessons from deployments help teams recognise patterns across customer engagements and decide what should happen next.
> 
> Explore how teams distinguish between problems that need a local fix and those worth turning into reusable tools, how field experience can feed back into workflows, products or models, and how those decisions change as the underlying models become more capable. _(Technical Level: 100–200)_
